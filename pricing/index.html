Forestry Mulching vs. Full Land Clearing

Forestry Mulching

One machine grinds underbrush, vines and small trees in place and leaves a layer of mulch that protects the soil. Larger trees you want to keep stay standing, roots stay in the ground and there's nothing to haul off. Best for reclaiming overgrown acreage, opening up views, trails, fence lines and wildfire defensible space.

Full Land Clearing

Trees, stumps and roots are removed (grubbed) and the debris is hauled off or processed, leaving bare, buildable ground. It takes more equipment and time, so it typically costs two to three times as much per acre. Best for new home sites, driveways, pastures and construction pads.

Pricing FAQ

How much does land clearing cost?

Forestry mulching typically runs $1,000–$4,000 for up to 1 acre: underbrush and small trees are mulched in place and the mulch stays on the ground. Full land clearing, which removes trees, stumps and roots for a bare, buildable site, runs roughly $9,000–$15,000 per acre. Price depends on the type of work requested, the machines needed, the grade of the land and location. Not sure which you need? Call (865) 290-8815 for a free on-site estimate.

These are wide, ballpark ranges, not a quote. Multiple factors affect the final price, including density, terrain and grade, access points to the site, the scope of work, materials and location. For an in-depth quote, it’s best for us to come out and see the property in person, so we can make sure you’re not being overcharged.

How much does a patio or hardscape project cost?

Patios range in size and complexity. Full patio installs start around $8,000. An average patio (about 500 sq ft) with a fire pit and seat wall and limited customization ranges from $18,000 to $32,000 or more. Larger patios with customized elements — water features, outdoor kitchens, shade structures and planter boxes — can range from $35,000 to $100,000 or more.

These are wide, ballpark ranges, not a quote. Multiple factors affect the final price, including density, terrain and grade, access points to the site, the scope of work, materials and location.
