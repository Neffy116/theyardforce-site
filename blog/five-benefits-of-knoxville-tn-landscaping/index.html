Five Benefits of Knoxville, TN, Landscaping

January 30, 2025 · The Yard Force

You may think landscaping’s only benefit is making your yard prettier, but there’s much more to it than that. Landscaping and its close cousin, hardscaping, both offer an impressive array of perks for a Knoxville property. When done right, landscaping can even save you money.

Below, learn the top five benefits of landscaping for your Knoxville home. The experts at The Yard Force weigh in as local favorites for this type of work.

Raise Your Property Value and Curb Appeal

Suppose you’re cruising Knoxville looking for a house to buy. You come across a place that looks decent enough, but the yard, to put it mildly, is a mess. The grass is overgrown, there are no flowers or bushes to speak of, and the trees look like they haven’t been pruned in years, if ever.

Would you buy this house? You cannot underestimate the importance of good landscaping if you’re planning to put your home on the market at some point. Professional landscaping attracts more interested buyers and can even fetch a higher price for your home.

That’s not just speculation. As per the American Society of Landscape Architects, a home with professional landscaping can fetch up to 20% more upon sale!

Keep Your Property Cooler on Hot Summer Days

Knoxville enjoys fairly mild winter temperatures, but come summertime, the mercury can spike well into the 90s. You crank up the air conditioning to stay comfortable, but that cool air costs you in the form of sky-high energy bills. Wouldn’t it be great if there was a way to reduce those bills while still staying cool when the weather turns sultry? 

Landscaping can be part of the solution.

Trees, grass, and other vegetation absorb the sun’s scorching rays and can cool your property by several degrees on those hot days. Additionally, planting trees on the west and south sides of your home will provide much-needed shade during the hottest part of the day.

Reduce Your Water Bills With Xeriscaping

Tired of spending hundreds per month on your lawn? Sick of grappling with that irrigation system every spring? You’re a great candidate for xeriscaping, landscaping that requires little to no water. 

First Utility District’s restriction stipulates that homeowners can only water lawns three times a week, for example. Xeriscaping is extremely helpful for avoiding those frustrating water usage restrictions. It also looks good year-round in areas without much rainfall.

“Sure, xeriscaping sounds great. However, I don’t want my yard filled with nothing but cacti!” If you’re secretly thinking this, you’re not alone. Don’t worry; xeriscaping facilitates some highly impressive versatility in your plant choice. 

There’s only one criterion you’ll need to meet and that’s picking plants that are drought-resistant! Need ideas? Try these beauties:

  • Germander sage: Featuring lovely violet-tinted flowers, germander sage is an evergreen plant native to Mexico.
  • Big sagebrush: This plant is ideal for covering large areas with foliage quickly. Depending on how you grow it, big sagebrush functions as either a shrub or a tree that can reach up to 15 feet tall.
  • Ice plant: The ice plant’s blossoms come in several colors, including pink, orange, yellow, and purple. It’s native to Africa, making it perfect for xeriscape gardens.

Prevent Soil Erosion With Good Retaining Walls

Does the topography of your yard lend itself to pooling water after a rain? If so, it makes sense to invest in retaining walls made from concrete, brick, or some other type of stone. These walls keep soil in place and minimize the effects of erosion, but they can be just as beautiful as they are practical. 

Many homeowners like to add mulching and plant flowers between retaining wall pieces to add visual appeal to their properties. Why not double it as convenient seating, too?
